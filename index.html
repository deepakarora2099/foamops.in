Why plants ask for this

The numbers already exist on paper. They just never reach the books cleanly.

Today

Every pump line is retyped by hand

The in-charge copies kilograms from the printout into the production journal and splits each mix with a calculator: half calcium and half polyol for a 1:1 mix, one part amine and six parts polyol for 1:7.

Today

Yield shows up at month end

Water leaves as gas and the block loses weight while it cures. Without those losses worked out per batch, a bad pour hides inside the monthly stock difference.

Today

Cut sheets lose their batch

Blocks are cut days later. A-grade, B-grade, offcuts and trim are rarely tied back to the block, so nobody can say which recipe gives the best recovery.

Today

Pending orders live in WhatsApp

Orders arrive as messages and photos. Part shipments, short-closes and bundles waiting on foam are tracked in spreadsheets that are out of date by lunch.

How it works · one batch

Five steps from pour to posted journal

The yellow step is the only one that uses AI. Every number it reads is checked by plain arithmetic and confirmed by a person before anything is sent on.

Machine

Pour and print

The line pours the block and prints its batch report with set rate, actual rate and kilograms for each pump.

Foaming in-charge · phone

Scan

One photo. FoamOps reads recipe, times, block length and every pump line, and keeps the photo with the batch.

FoamOps

Check

Each pump: kg used against actual kg/min × run time, and actual rate against set rate. Anything outside your limits is flagged.

Floor

Weigh and cure

The block is weighed against its expected weight: chemicals less water, less curing loss. Yield goes on the batch card.

Accounts

Post

Pumps map to raw-material items, mixes are split, kraft paper added per block size. The journal goes to your accounting software.

Phone · foaming in-charge

Scan, confirm, save

The in-charge takes one photo after each pour. FoamOps fills the consumption lines and only stops them where a number needs a second look.

  • Mixes split automatically: the 1:1 calcium-polyol and 1:7 amine-polyol mixes become their two items.
  • Water is recorded as gas loss, not as a raw material.
  • Kraft paper is added from the block size: top, bottom and sides.
  • If the photo can't be read, the same screen accepts typed values.

Owner · Notepad or the settings screen

Your limits, in a file you can read

Every amber and red check in FoamOps reads its limit from one settings file. The owner changes a number, the app picks it up within a minute, and the change is logged.

  • 19 limits across foaming, cutting, offcuts, pending orders and returns.
  • Override a limit for one product only.
  • A mistyped value is ignored and the last good value stays.

AI scan

AI reads the photo. Arithmetic decides if it's right.

Printouts get folded, smudged and photographed at an angle. FoamOps doesn't trust a reading because a model produced it. It trusts a reading because the numbers agree with each other.

Reads

Recipe, batch number, start and end time, block length, and each pump's set rate, actual rate and kilograms.Works from a normal phone photo taken on the floor.

Cross-checks

kg used ≈ actual kg/min × production time, for every pump.A misread digit breaks the equation and gets flagged with the corrected value.

Flags

Pumps running off their set rate by more than your amber or red limit.A red flag needs a note before the batch can be saved.

Keeps

The original photo with the batch, next to what was read and who confirmed it.Auditors and the owner see the same evidence.

Falls back

No internet, no reading? The in-charge types the values on the same screen.Production never waits for the AI.
